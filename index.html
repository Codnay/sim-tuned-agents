Sim-Tuned Robot Agents

Deepanshu Rohilla Pranay Gupta

Argon Robotics

CoRL 2026 Late-Breaking Results

Abstract

Frontier models can now look at a scene, plan and write code, but getting one to do a contact-rich task on a real robot is still hard. Most of the work is not in the model but in what surrounds it: the commands it can call, the tools it can use and what it is told about the task. Tuning this harness on a real robot is slow, costly and risky. We tune it in a physically measured simulation of the real cell instead. A frontier model, Gemini 4 Argon, drives two simulated arms through a bimanual screwdriving task, and between rounds we change its tools, written skills and lessons based on where it fails. We then move the same harness to the real robot without changing the model. The tuned harness takes the model much further through the task than plain commands, both in simulation and on the real robot, where it picks up the screwdriver, seats it on the screw and drives the screw.

The task

Two YAM arms share one table, with a camera on top and one on each wrist. The model works only from these cameras and has five steps to finish:

  1. Pick up the screw.
  2. Place it in the pilot hole of the pine box.
  3. Pick up the screwdriver.
  4. Place the screwdriver bit on top of the screw.
  5. Drive the screw in.
